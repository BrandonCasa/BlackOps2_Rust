# Realtime rendering and material migration

Bevy 0.19.1 owns the renderer. `src/rendering/opaque.rs` extends StandardMaterial for authored terrain layers, tile core/coating coverage and animated water. `pbr.rs` assigns canonical maps to standard skeletal/equipment materials. Prepared version-2 DDS channels preserve complete material-aware mip chains, source sampler dispositions, coverage and authored UV/normal transforms. Color is intrinsic reflectance; response R is neutral rather than native baked occlusion. GGX fitting and family classification remain practical material approximations.

Native directional lightmaps, per-instance irradiance streams, SH and captured reflection cubes are retired from the runtime pack and shaders. Original input bytes remain optional source data. `tools/realtime_assets.py` strips native lighting metadata and compacts orphan streams; preparation omits them and the importer rejects them. Tile control magnitudes are normalized into material coverage rather than multiplying incident macro darkness.

`sky.rs` installs Earth Rayleigh/Mie sky, a smooth exponential height-density fog volume, shadowed volumetric sunlight and Ultra current-frame SSAO. Finite geometry uses the shadowed fog volume alone: Bevy's unobstructed planetary aerial LUT and constant volume ambient scatter previously layered sky-colored haze over enclosed surfaces. The physical sky and its transport input remain. The embedded profile supplies a neutral 80-unit diffuse readability fill through the native material BRDF and AO; ambient specular is excluded because reflections come from the traced scene. This fill is an explicit artistic approximation, independent of probe availability or direct sun visibility. The directional sun and warm shadowed lanterns illuminate world and first-person layers. Lantern receiver offsets are bounded for thin walls. World skeletons, grenades, mines and other equipment retain normal shadow casting; overlay rigs and luminous lamp geometry are excluded. No temporal reconstruction is introduced.

`sky_lighting.rs` gates sky environment filtering at the configured fixed rate while keeping the same full-resolution output handles. A changed sun, atmosphere medium or camera height refreshes it immediately and again after atmosphere generation. Local scene reflection filtering remains current-frame. Visible sky and shadowed height fog remain current-frame. Unchanged lantern shadow faces retain the existing joint-pose, caster, light and texture-slot cache. Directional cascades always execute Bevy's native camera-owned passes: suspending their views retained incorrect occlusion in the spawn room even when blocker meshes were present. They preserve Bevy's texel-snapped XY rows and the fixed world-anchored depth slab. Directional PCSS converts blocker separation into world distance and anchors filter noise in the sun plane; perspective spotlight filtering retains Bevy's native path. Sun receiver depth bias is two millimeters and normal bias is one quarter texel. OpaqueSurface native shadow specialization renders both sides of room-facing sheets for sun/lantern maps; camera depth and visible raster culling, normals and alpha tests retain authored semantics. [Unity's shadow guidance](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/ShadowPerformance.html) documents bias-induced bleeding and two-sided shadow casters. [Epic's Lumen technical guidance](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lumen-technical-details-in-unreal-engine) distinguishes inadequate blocker representations and thin software distance fields; the hardware triangle path here already tests both faces, so arbitrary wall thickening was not adopted. Focused window evidence and the user-requested validation skips are recorded in the current handoff.

`static_gi.rs` implements one shared hardware DDGI field using Solari's scene infrastructure, without its replacement lighting pipeline. Three world-aligned toroidal cascades retain overlapping history and spread refresh work across frames, prioritizing new regions and moving receivers. Each probe stores 8x8 directional irradiance, packed 16x16 distance moments, classification and bounded relocation. Accumulation is in world space, with no screen history. The adaptation follows [NVIDIA RTXGI-DDGI](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/RTXGI-DDGI) at f33e496ca31b3f0eec1c4e2cbaa8bb620e337fa6; attribution and its license are retained. Native per-view probe gathering supplies the same diffuse GI to world and first-person PBR receivers. Blocked probe weights have no light floor; initialized support and visibility confidence are separate, preventing normalization of an occluded neighborhood into full brightness. The finest supported cascade owns its interior, including darkness; coarse fields blend only at its boundary. Receiver bias is capped at two centimeters, and nearer visibility moments are admitted without the old hysteresis delay. The superseded custom SSGI pass is removed. SSAO remains separate contact occlusion, and DDGI does not replace specular reflections.

The ray scene now includes shared static providers, visible rigid actors/equipment and posed skeletal providers using the native Solari BLAS/TLAS. Immutable bind data is shared; invalid, missing or hidden poses are excluded. Companion surface attributes preserve authored layer UVs, coverage, blend operations, tile core/coating masks, normal rotations, response, emissive and sidedness. Bounded continuation rejects masked holes; transparent surfaces remain excluded from this opaque transport approximation. Single-layer vertices share a neutral record instead of retaining unused layer controls.

Posed providers publish before native asset-event flushing. A surface invalidates only when matrices influencing its indexed vertices change, even when several material surfaces share a complete skeleton accessor. Generator players and enemy attachment sockets are discovered once at scene readiness; animation graphs are shared and unchanged controls/transforms do not trigger redundant work. Native CPU animation and GPU raster skinning retain their existing ownership. Texture range reads remain bounded asynchronous I/O; poses remain current-frame rather than delayed across frames.

A camera-centered six-face scene cube traces those same contributors and materials every frame, shades local sun/lantern visibility and shared DDGI, and supplies Bevy's filtered GGX environment maps to both raster views. World receivers use native probe parallax correction; the overlay uses the camera-origin map. Outside the local box or an empty native cluster, the scene cube also supplies the remaining reflection weight; an unoccluded sky fallback cannot bypass world geometry. A bounded glossy continuation approximates secondary radiance without screen-space history. This is a local reflection probe approximation, not per-pixel multi-bounce path tracing. Material appearance, dynamic contributions and convergence still require visual qualification. Volumetric cloud weather is not implemented.

An exact finite-range lamp grid avoids scanning every lantern at every ray hit, with complete-list fallback outside its bounded grid. Lamp/grid buffers and cube views retain their native resources while inputs remain unchanged. The producer runs before Bevy environment downsampling/filtering, so the raster views can consume the current cube. Reflection rays use authored dielectric F0, geometric offsets and hemisphere-safe mapped normals. Companion material channels own their maps; native ray carrier materials omit duplicate physical bindings.

`ray_surface.rs` reads frame-stamped GPU atomic minima from both producers and submits their full-chain mip demand through shared stable Image IDs. Raster visibility, shadow coverage and hit footprints use the same disk-backed streaming system. Canonical assembly keeps geometry and full source DDS chains, emits bootstrap tails and validates the dependency closure. Native material/scene extraction follows actual changes; resident image-view changes refresh native bindings so old views retire. A bounded settling refresh adopts asynchronously compacted native BLAS, then unchanged scenes retain their bindings.

Ray footprints project onto the actual triangle plane and use a complete world-to-UV Jacobian. Each map's rectangular extent converts the footprint to a texel ellipse; explicit gradients enable native anisotropy while full-chain feedback accounts for resident tails. Filtered maps use trilinear mip filtering and 16x anisotropy; authored point/no-mip maps retain their disposition. Managed image payloads transfer to RenderWorld rather than retaining CPU mip copies. Promotions and releases precede asset-event flushing. Normal/response mip changes and ray-only meshes cannot invalidate raster shadows; coverage changes invalidate their actual caster users.

F11 cycles lit, identity, roughness, metallic, normal, coverage and isolated runtime IBL diagnostics. Diagnostics use the pinned Bevy lighting equations. They assist appearance inspection and are not performance measurements.

The world camera retains linear HDR until the first-person view is composited. The final view owns AgX, grading, bloom and SMAA, so those operations apply once to the composed image. Native Bevy histogram auto-exposure meters that linear image, targeting photographic middle gray and bounding adaptation with the authored indoor/outdoor EV limits. Both raster cameras use the same fixed exposure scale before metering. The retired overhead-collider test forced dark-room exposure inside bright ruins and washed out their colors. Exposure adaptation retains only a scalar metering state, not screen-space image history.

The final display write replaces its target without reblending alpha over the world output. Startup bakes a global artistic look into a cloned native AgX 32-cubed LUT: perceptual contrast, muted-color vibrance, neutral shadows and warm highlights. It adds no runtime image-filter pass or camera-position override. Compact energy-conserving bloom preserves local texture contrast, and native SMAA Ultra retains current-frame edge detail. Bevy's visibility-bitmask AO remains spatially filtered; no temporal reconstruction, sharpen halo or motion blur is introduced.

Native Solari installs private systems during plugin finish, while pipelined-rendering cleanup moves RenderApp into its render thread. The integration finalizer runs between those stages and attaches scene/material refresh conditions before schedule initialization. Bevy diagnostic names remain enabled in ordinary optimized builds so the pinned integration contract is identical in profiling and production. DDGI and reflection production, native downsampling/filtering and camera rendering execute in native root-graph submission order, with GPU diagnostics covering each producer.

Bevy's native multithreaded schedules, pipelined RenderApp, GPU mesh preprocessing and GPU raster skinning retain their ownership. Navigation validates independent nodes and directed links on the existing ComputeTaskPool, then assembles the same authored order; physics and gameplay do not move to a later frame. A typed native audio asset decodes short repeated Vorbis clips on AsyncComputeTaskPool and shares immutable PCM with standard Bevy spatial sinks. Its cache owns at most 64 MiB after maintenance, with an 8 MiB per-clip limit; active sinks may retain evicted clips. Long music retains compressed streaming. These are CPU task-pool changes, not asynchronous GPU queue execution.

Performance evidence follows [Bevy profiling.md](https://docs.rs/crate/bevy/0.19.1/source/docs/profiling.md): release Tracy/Chrome CPU traces, Tracy GPU zones or a vendor GPU profiler, and Tracy allocation profiling for memory. Matched 1920x1080 allocation-instrumented stationary captures measured 184.927 to 77.498 ms mean frame time; moving captures measured 230.534 to 99.332 ms. Unchanged native ray bindings and point visibility lists are reused. Tracked heap peaks fell 2.87 and 0.95 percent, while allocation counts per frame fell 70.30 and 68.36 percent. Allocated bytes per stationary frame rose 5.86 percent; stationary p99 and moving worst-frame latency also worsened. GPU readback denominators differ, nested timings overlap, and DDGI probe work follows a fixed wall-time cadence. Thread elapsed time includes waits, tracked allocations exclude process RAM/VRAM, and RenderDoc replay is not production latency. Generator startup/restart workload is reported separately. The current handoff distinguishes assembled assets, compiled source, actual rendering and measured results.

Final ordinary 1920x1080 moving qualification selected native GPU frustum/indirect batching with CPU admission retained: 46.972 FPS / 21.289 ms mean versus Direct 40.049 FPS / 24.969 ms, and two-phase occlusion 32.164 FPS / 31.090 ms. World occlusion is therefore disabled. Point/spot shadow phases use native Direct preprocessing, while auxiliary current-view guards skip empty indirect work. Directional cascades remain native camera children; assigning independent root graphs duplicated preprocessing and real shadow draws. Native pending specializations and texture-slot ownership remain conservative. All six three-layer generator effects were admitted, both closing screenshots rendered, and normal exit had no GPU/readback errors. Whole-run process RAM sampling includes startup and rotation and establishes no RAM gain. Final RenderDoc and full generator/outdoor/combat requalification are deferred at the user's wrap-up request. Profiling builds use the native Tracy only-localhost feature and the driver sets the same environment option for preserved binaries; no firewall authorization is required. The current handoff and todo.md retain all deferred work.
