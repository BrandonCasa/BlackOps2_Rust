# Standard rendering

The previous viewer depended on captured draw membership, translated native shaders, frozen lighting/resources and camera patches. It admitted 6,296 of 6,469 world surfaces and omitted static model rendering. That is unsuitable for an arbitrary player camera.

The game loads the complete world and all placed static models through standard glTF. Bevy owns PBR, transparency, shadows, bounds, culling, cameras and post-processing. Normals, source tangents/handedness, RGBA vertex colors, UV seams and world secondary UVs survive preparation. An opaque shadow-helper surface receives collision disposition without being drawn as a visible wall.

Current practical material conversion reconstructs BC5 normal Z, flips DirectX normal Y and maps gloss alpha to roughness without clamping its range. Most surfaces are dielectric. All available layers and raw specular RGB/gloss alpha survive as PNGs referenced by compact surface metadata. Alpha-tested surfaces use masks; blended/special surfaces and water use standard approximations. Both lightmap channels, all probe faces/coefficients and world/static assignments remain retained. HDR, AgX, bloom and distance fog are camera components.

The shared model tile/macro family now receives an offline 2048-square diffuse bake instead of stretching its first detail texture across the mesh. Two repeating surface textures are weighted in linear space by the macro map's red/green channels, retaining their occlusion magnitude. This applies by source technique family to pillars, rubble, rocks and statues, without object-name or placement overrides. Four repeats per authored UV tile are a reconstruction choice; source detail maps remain at their decoded resolution. The third color binding is a control map, not displayable albedo. Raw inputs remain retained and `surface.preparation` identifies the bake. Other world layer families still select their first color/normal layer and need secondary UV/blend evaluation. No albedo smoothing is applied.

The reference-room appearance pass decodes the secondary atlas's ambient and directional RGB/alpha bands into a shared linear Bevy lightmap, using ambient plus 0.35 times directional radiance. This diffuse average is a practical substitute for the original direction/normal-dependent third-band response; radiance above four is clipped by the runtime UNORM representation. It no longer samples the packed three-band atlas as an ordinary color image. Sunlight diffuse contributions are disabled for lightmapped world geometry; a restrained cool ambient fill keeps the practical reconstruction readable. Unmapped models retain cool ambient/sun lighting.

Placed material names containing `glass_on` receive warm emissive glass and local Bevy point lights at the glass mesh bounds center. Emissive glass does not cast a shadow over its own light. Shadows are enabled within ten meters of the camera; lights remain attached to source placements. Runtime copies of converted normal maps use 35 percent XY strength and reconstructed Z to reduce the over-sharpened concrete/pillar response. Concrete materials use a matte 0.9 roughness instead of the first-layer gloss approximation. Prepared source channels and shared geometry remain untouched. Cool fog, restrained sunlight, bloom and a smaller lower-screen HUD support the reference's warm lamps/cool recesses. Controls remain available when paused.

Camera exposure smoothly moves between indoor EV100 7 and Bevy's default EV100 9.7 outdoors. An upward Avian ray checks for cover within twenty meters, excluding the player. This is a practical exposure transition, not reconstruction of the original map's authored exposure volumes.

This is APPROXIMATE. Layer mixing, source reflection probes, exact native directional lightmaps, animated water, particles and special visual families need later work in the same game. Lantern intensity/range and normal strength are visual reconstruction choices. The temporary pistol remains temporary. Captured-frame equality is not an acceptance requirement. Evaluate coherent appearance and complete geometry from moving/distant views.

Release surveys account for the complete source geometry but expose rough vegetation alpha, debug-colored surfaces and helper-like shapes in some locations. These are unresolved conversion/disposition defects; successful loading and round progression do not establish complete appearance.
