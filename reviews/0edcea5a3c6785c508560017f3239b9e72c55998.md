## Findings

### P2 - Actor penetration thickness can be the entry into another hit volume rather than the exit from the struck body

- **Original code:** `src/hit_regions.rs:183-192`; hit-volume construction and initial-hit selection at `:48-87`.
- **Reference:** `t6zm-master/exports/functions/005d/005d07a0.c:133-158,169-218` performs forward/reverse tracing and computes the distance between paired penetration boundaries before deducting thickness/depth. The 1-inch floor is literal at `:195-198`. Its 100-unit depth branches are at `:117-127,214-218`.
- **Trigger:** A penetrating shot intersects more than one enabled capsule on the same actor, including a diagonal torso/limb shot or overlapping skeletal hit regions.
- **Current behavior and impact:** After selecting the actor's first hit, the new code starts 0.002 metres beyond that point and raycasts every enabled volume with `solid=false`, then takes the smallest distance. A volume containing that start point contributes its exit; a different volume that does not contain it contributes its entry. The code does not retain the struck volume or distinguish those cases. It can consequently debit only the distance to a nearer unrelated boundary, even though the ray remains in the actor beyond it. The fallback of one inch also hides an unresolved exit. This changes the damage multiplier reaching subsequent zombies and can make body penetration depend on hit-volume subdivision rather than the actual traversed body thickness. The native export's paired trace does not justify this minimum-over-arbitrary-volumes calculation.
- **Future correction:** Track a valid entry/exit interval through the actor's supported collision representation. When using several hit volumes, combine overlapping occupied intervals and distinguish outside entries from inside exits; apply the native minimum thickness after establishing that interval. Do not use the first intersection with an unrelated capsule as a body exit.
- **Limit:** The exact native actor trace geometry and all trace-flag meanings are not reconstructed here. The Rust boundary-selection defect is established statically; its frequency and quantitative gameplay effect require later real-game validation.

### P2 - ADS recentering modifiers are applied to hip-derived recoil recovery in every aiming state

- **Original code:** `src/weapons.rs:825-829`; the scalar is populated from the hip field by `tools/prepare_arsenal.py:174-175` (read only).
- **Reference:** `solo-content/source/attachment/grip:1`, `acog:1`, and `silencer:1` specify `adsViewKickCenterSpeedScale` values 1.02, 1.02, and 0.98. `solo-content/source/weapons/hamr_upgraded_zm:1` distinguishes `hipViewKickCenterSpeed=900` from `adsViewKickCenterSpeed=550`; `dsr50_upgraded_zm:1` distinguishes 550 from 750. Native `t6zm-master/exports/functions/008e/008e9f30.c:10-19` multiplies the attachment field at offset 0x5c, and its caller `functions/0052/00521290.c:48-84` selects separate branches according to the aiming fraction before invoking this accessor.
- **Trigger:** Hip-fire or leave ADS with a grip/ACOG gun, or compare a suppressed DSR's hip and fully aimed recovery.
- **Current behavior and impact:** Removing the invented grip kick reduction is appropriate, but its replacement unconditionally multiplies the one `recoil_recovery` value, even when `self.ads == 0`. That value was imported from the hip centering field. The new ADS-specific attachment scale therefore also changes hip-fire recovery, while fully aimed recovery still starts from the hip field instead of the distinct native ADS field. For a HAMR, multiplying its hip-derived 9.0 by 1.02 is not the same as applying the recovered scale to the ADS source value 550 under the existing unit conversion. The exact native recovery integrator is more involved than this subtraction, but the wrong field/state composition is independently visible in the source and asset definitions.
- **Future correction:** Preserve separate native hip/ADS centering parameters and apply attachment scales in the supported aiming branch, including the appropriate transition behavior. Keep kick impulse, centering speed, and damping separate rather than compensating one with the other.
